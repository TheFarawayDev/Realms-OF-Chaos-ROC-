        03 / WHAT IS IN IT

        Features

        Everything below is in the current build, on both loaders.

        1

        The trial

        One god waits on every floor. Beat it to go up.

        Signature moves

        Every god has a move of its own - Zeus calls down the Wrath of Olympus, Hermes windsteps behind you, Hades opens the Gates of Tartarus. Each is named on screen before it lands.

        Three phases

        And the floor itself is a weapon: shockwaves to jump, fissures that split it in a star, a wall that sweeps the arena with one gap - each lit on the ground before it lands. At 65% a god raises its aegis: nothing hurts it until the shield pylons round the floor are broken. At 30% comes its wrath - the floor is lost ring by ring, and strikes fall from above, each marked before it lands.

        Gods that learn

        A god measures your weapons, their enchantments and your armour, and rises to match. Shoot it and it turns shots aside and closes in; stay hand to hand and its guard lashes back. Each phase changes how it looks.

        Gods that remember

        Habits carry from trial to trial. Keep shooting from afar, hiding behind a shield, stopping to heal or staying out of reach, and the next god has an answer ready. Fight differently and it is forgotten.

        Parry, dodge and riposte

        Tap sneak, or raise a shield, just as a blow lands and it is turned aside: the attacker reels and your next blow is a riposte. Hold a direction as you tap and you dodge instead, dashing clear. Blows landed without being hit build momentum. A parried bolt goes back to whoever loosed it.

        Fair fights

        Big attacks are telegraphed, and the ground breaks under them. Enraged gods make the floor erupt under your feet. Beaten a god? Call a rematch.

        Living floors

        Every floor has its own terrain - and it moves. Wound a god and the ground shifts under you: pits rise into daises, halls fold into terraces, then settle back.

        The tower adapts

        More challengers make a harder trial, the world's difficulty sets its pace, and a god that keeps beating you relents a little.

        2

        The passages

        Between two floors lies a maze to get through.

        One to ten levels

        Every passage is one to ten levels deep, each with a name like 7-B2 and each harder than the last. The next level is built while you explore this one.

        Puzzle rooms

        Vaults of lights to put out, chimes to play back in order, and trembling floors with one safe path across. Beam rooms are laid out afresh every time: up to five mirrors to set, decoys that lead nowhere, and only so many turns before the room throws them all about again.

        Trap halls

        Forty-six halls that do not say what they are: a plain floor, a few stains, somebody's bones - and a wisp over a tile the moment before it goes. Fire in rows, frost from the walls inwards, lightning that strikes where you stood, one narrow path that shows itself once. Small, ordinary or wide, harder the deeper, and named the first time one bites you. Also: Crusher halls, dart halls, rune chambers that flood - and halls that never rest: spikes by the colour of the tile, fire that walks the rows, a blade from wall to wall, an arc that turns about a pillar, gas that fills half the room at a time. Some corridors have jets. Each shows what it is about to do.

        No two rooms alike

        Levels are laid out like a stronghold: no doorway joins two rooms of the same kind, and barred cells and stacked storerooms stand among the halls. Rooms are low, tall or great; some a step wider; some with cut corners, a ring of columns or a sunken floor. Nearly every level has a stairwell: two flights up to a loft, and a cache on it.

        Corridors that wind

        Pillared galleries and causeways over water open up between the rooms. Most corridors do not run straight: they go round a baffle that hides what is ahead, or pass a nook with an urn at the back of it. The maze turns more and has fewer short cuts.

        Work fast, and remember

        Rune chambers shut you in and get worse by the moment: one floods, one is a furnace that shows its order only once, one goes dark and wants its order pressed backwards.

        Lesser gods

        Twelve lesser gods - Eris, Nemesis, Styx, Lethe and more - hold the arenas of the passages, and each keeps a piece of armour from the old stories.

        Afflicted levels

        Deeper in, most levels carry an affliction - gloom, weight, frailty, hunger or fatigue - laid on everyone who stays. A shrine holds it off for a while.

        Sentinels

        Seventy-five kinds, twenty-five to a tower. Shield-bearers shelter their allies, wardens take their wounds, singers raise the fallen - while bombers, snipers, thieves, duelists, reavers and slammers make trouble. They draw back before a heavy blow: step away, or parry it. A bestiary keeps count of what you fell.

        Find your way

        A chart with fog of war inks in each room as you walk into it, with a sign for every seal, cache and gate. A shrine shows you the whole level. The gate itself faces the doorway you come in by.

        A boon from every seal

        Break a seal and take one of three boons - more health, a harder blow, quicker feet, a longer moment to parry in. They stack, and last until the passage is behind you.

        Different in every world

        Passages are generated from your world's seed - where the rooms connect, where the seals hide, what each room holds. A passage seals itself again for anyone who has not walked it.

        3

        The world

        The realms have to be found before they can be climbed.

        Find the way in

        Three citadels, far apart, keep the keystones: castles held by half-kin, with a fallen Titan waiting on the roof of the keep - Hyperion, Oceanus or Cronus, each with four moves of its own. The gates were broken on purpose. Follow your compass to a Ruined Gate in the Overworld, track down three keystones in shrines across the world, and wake it.

        Scan, don't warp

        Nothing teleports you in an instant. The compass scans for a minute or more - rough readings, a Clean Lock, a burst of static. Raise relay towers to steady it, and waygates to travel between after a five-second channel.

        A living, growing hub

        The Oracle's flame burns at its heart and echoes of the gods watch from their gates. Hand in what it asks for and the hub rises through five tiers - a sanctuary that mends you, a daily bounty board, faster waygates.

        A story to uncover

        Chaos is unmaking the world, and the gods who hid from it have forgotten why. The Oracle tells it in nineteen chapters, written as you climb - and every god you beat is not slain, but woken.

        4

        Your climb

        Progress is your own, alone or with friends.

        The panoply

        Eight pieces of armour, each with one power: the Helm of Hades hides you while you sneak, the Talaria let you leap and land soft, the Aegis gives a second wind. Won from lesser gods - or commissioned from the forge, which makes any relic too, slowly.

        Mastery

        Every god woken and every passage walked earns a point for the mastery tree: twelve skills across combat, survival and exploration, re-spendable at any time.

        Climb together

        Form a party of up to four and enter a tower on your highest member's floor. Nobody climbs past a god they have not beaten - but friends can always come and help.

        Its own screen

        Every screen has a look of its own - the Oracle's chronicle on a night sky, the bounty board on planks and cork, the handbook in stitched leather. The Codex, the handbook, the chronicle and the altar open as pages on a stone tablet drawn for the mod, with their own buttons and pictures - not a stock dialog. Every menu is a real screen of its own, like an inventory, and the HUD is drawn by the game itself: one panel for a god, a plaque for the floor or passage you are on. Tips are small cards, never a title across the view; the cast bar that names a god's move is yours to turn on.

        A handbook, not a wiki

        A handbook opens for new players, and a short tip appears the first time each new thing happens. No chat spam: menus, cards and a plaque instead - and nothing is said twice in a row.

        04 / WHO WAITS

        The gods

        One god per floor, each with its own signature move. Pick a tower.

        05 / ON SCREEN

        In game

        Menus and on-screen prompts, not chat spam.

        Codex of Chaos

        Your whole journey in one menu: the three towers, every floor and god, what is defeated and what is next. Click a floor you have reached to travel there, go to the hub, or leave the realms.

        Trial Altar

        Each floor's altar opens its own screen: who waits on this floor, and one button that begins the trial - or, once they have fallen, sends you up.

        Tower HUD

        A bar at the top of the screen always shows which tower and floor you are on. Arrivals, trials and victories appear as titles; short notices sit above the hotbar.

        06 / SET UP

        Install

        Four steps, about five minutes.

        Java and a loader

        Install Java 25, then Fabric or Forge for Minecraft 26.3.

        Fabric only

        Add Fabric API to your mods folder. Forge needs nothing extra.

        Drop in the jar

        Put the downloaded .jar in your mods folder and start the game.

        Join a world

        You are given the Codex, the Compass of Chaos and the Oracle's letter. Follow the compass to a Ruined Gate.
